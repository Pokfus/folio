# Sources register

Every citation, once **verified**, in its final form — so a work wanted by six cards is verified once and
pasted six times, and the formatting cannot drift between them. Not shipped; not loaded by the site.

Two sections, and the line between them is the whole point of the file.

Each citation ends in an **access label**, `[Open access]` or `[Paywalled]`, which is part of the stored
string and renders after the link. A paywalled work is citable only when it is the **landmark defining
paper** for the claim; the bulk of any card's list must be open. Where a landmark paper is paywalled and
its abstract carries the claim, "opened" below means the publisher's public abstract — recorded as such,
never silently passed off as the full text.

## VERIFIED

### harmand-2015 · Lomekwi 3
Sonia Harmand et al., "3.3-Million-Year-Old Stone Tools from Lomekwi 3, West Turkana, Kenya," *Nature* 521,
no. 7552 (2015): 310–15, https://hal.science/hal-04379924. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · **publisher PDF** deposited in HAL (journal pagination intact — the DOI itself is
  paywalled, so the HAL record is the link a reader can actually use)
- supports: 3.3 Ma and "oldest stone tools yet known" (310, title + abstract); ~19 artefacts in situ and
  ~130 surface/slope, 149 total (310); dating by the Toroto Tuff at 3.31 ± 0.02 Ma plus magnetostratigraphy
  in the Mammoth reverse subchron (311); the core-and-flake refit showing knapping on the spot (311);
  83 cores, 35 flakes, 7 anvils, 7 percussors, and anvils "up to 15 kg" with flat stabilizing surfaces
  (312); passive-hammer and bipolar technique vs Oldowan free-hand percussion (312, 314); *K. platyops*
  the only hominin known in West Turkana at the time, *A. afarensis* in the Lower Awash (313); the
  Lomekwian named and dated 700 kyr before the Oldowan (310, 314); earliest *Homo* at 2.8 Ma, note added
  in proof (314)
- used by: wh-014, wh-005

### dominguez-rodrigo-alcala-2016 · the Lomekwi critique
Manuel Domínguez-Rodrigo and Luis Alcalá, "3.3-Million-Year-Old Stone Tools and Butchery Traces? More
Evidence Needed," *PaleoAnthropology* 2016 (2016): 46–53, esp. 49 and 51,
https://paleoanthro.org/media/journal/content/PA20160046.pdf. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · publisher PDF (the journal's DOI 404s; the journal's own file URL is the stable one)
- supports: that the LOM3 assemblage was not shown to lie undisturbed in Pliocene ground, and that
  weathering could account for some of the fractures (49, under "Age Determination for the LOM3 Site" and
  the paragraphs above it); conclusions that the evidence is "insufficient to claim an anthropogenic agency
  and a Pliocene age" (51)
- used by: wh-014

### plummer-2025 · the Lomekwian reviewed
Thomas W. Plummer, Sonia Harmand, Emma M. Finestone, and Evan P. Wilson, "The First Million Years of
Technology: The Lomekwian and the Early Oldowan," *Annual Review of Anthropology* 54 (2025): 359–75, under
"Context and Characteristics of the Lomekwian Industry," https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10631785. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · full text via the **NSF Public Access Repository**, an official US government
  record (the publisher's own site blocks automated access; located by section, since the accessible copy
  carries online-first pagination)
- supports: that the in-situ controversy is live and has been replied to; that LOM3 is still the only
  excavated Lomekwian site
- used by: wh-014

### hublin-2017 · Jebel Irhoud, the fossils
Jean-Jacques Hublin et al., "New Fossils from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco and the Pan-African Origin of *Homo
sapiens*," *Nature* 546, no. 7657 (2017): 289–92, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22336. [Paywalled]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · **publisher abstract only** — the landmark paper for the find; no open deposit of
  the full text exists (the one repository copy is a submitted version behind a captcha)
- supports: the mosaic of modern facial, mandibular and dental morphology with a primitive neurocranium
  and endocranium; "the oldest and richest African Middle Stone Age hominin site that documents early
  stages of the *H. sapiens* clade"; that the processes behind our emergence "involved the whole African
  continent"
- used by: wh-045

### richter-2017 · Jebel Irhoud, the date
Daniel Richter et al., "The Age of the Hominin Fossils from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco, and the Origins of the
Middle Stone Age," *Nature* 546, no. 7657 (2017): 293–96, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22335. [Paywalled]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · **publisher abstract only** — the landmark dating paper; no open copy exists
- supports: thermoluminescence on fire-heated flints; a weighted average of 315 ± 34 ka; the previous
  earliest *H. sapiens* at ~195 ka and the conventional ~200 ka, which is where the card's "some 100,000
  years older" comes from; **"one of the earliest directly dated Middle Stone Age assemblages"** — which
  is *not* "the oldest well-dated assemblage anywhere", and the card was corrected to match
- used by: wh-045

### neubauer-2018 · brain shape
Simon Neubauer, Jean-Jacques Hublin, and Philipp Gunz, "The Evolution of Modern Human Brain Shape,"
*Science Advances* 4, no. 1 (2018): eaao5961, table 1 and "Gradual Globularization within *H. sapiens*,"
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5783678/. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · full text (CC BY-NC) via PMC
- supports: that the Jebel Irhoud braincases "are not globular"; Irhoud 1 and 2 at 315 ky with endocranial
  volumes inside the present-day range (table 1); that brain shape "evolved gradually within the *H.
  sapiens* lineage, reaching present-day human variation between about 100,000 and 35,000 years ago" —
  i.e. the modern face came first and the round skull later
- used by: wh-045

### meneganzin-2022 · the pan-African debate
Andra Meneganzin, Telmo Pievani, and Giorgio Manzi, "Pan-Africanism vs. Single-Origin of *Homo sapiens*:
Putting the Debate in the Light of Evolutionary Biology," *Evolutionary Anthropology* 31, no. 4 (2022):
199–212, under "Challenges and Implications of Jebel Irhoud,"
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9540121/. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · full text via PMC
- supports: the site's discovery during 1960s mining and the long-contested reading of the fossils; that a
  more modern-like face preceded the globular braincase; the pan-African picture of a population spread
  across the continent. Also flags that calling these "the oldest *Homo sapiens* fossils" is too hasty —
  which is why the card says "assigned to", not "of"
- used by: wh-045

### conard-2009 · the Venus of Hohle Fels
Nicholas J. Conard, "A Female Figurine from the Basal Aurignacian of Hohle Fels Cave in Southwestern
Germany," *Nature* 459, no. 7244 (2009): 248–52, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07995. [Paywalled]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · **publisher abstract only** — the defining publication for the object; no open copy
- supports: the figurine's discovery in the basal Aurignacian of Hohle Fels and its publication in
  *Nature*; the deliberate exaggeration of the sexual features (quoted verbatim in Morriss-Kay 2013, §3)
- used by: wh-069

### morriss-kay-2013 · the figurine described
Gillian Morriss-Kay, "A New Hypothesis on the Creation of the Hohle Fels “Venus” Figurine,"
*Palethnologie* 5 (2013), paras. 2–6, https://doi.org/10.4000/palethnologie.5135. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · full text on OpenEdition; numbered paragraphs are the locator
- supports: excavated in 2008 and "the oldest known piece of figurative 'art'", with charcoal around it
  dated 36,000–40,000 calendar years (§2); large protruding breasts, enlarged vulva, widely separated legs
  and "a well-worn loop carved in place of her head, suggesting that it was strung and suspended as a
  pendant" (§3); the transverse striations engraved into the ivory (§4); the absence of a head and the
  loop polished by wear (§6); the reading of the carving as a woman's own view of her body (§4–6);
  Gravettian Venus figurines at 24–29 ky "from France to Siberia" (§1)
- used by: wh-069

### floss-2015 · the Swabian Aurignacian
Harald Floss, "The Oldest Portable Art: The Aurignacian Ivory Figurines from the Swabian Jura (Southwest
Germany)," *Palethnologie* 7 (2015), paras. 7 and 13,
https://doi.org/10.4000/palethnologie.888. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · full text on OpenEdition
- supports: that the female figurine came from the **lowermost** Aurignacian level, and that the Swabian
  early Aurignacian dates to around 42,000 cal BP on the radiocarbon record (§7) — which is what "at least
  35,000, probably closer to 40,000" rests on; that the figurine and the vulture-bone flute lay in the same
  sub-layer centimetres apart (§13)
- used by: wh-069

### weber-2022 · the Venus of Willendorf
Gerhard W. Weber et al., "The Microstructure and the Origin of the Venus from Willendorf," *Scientific
Reports* 12 (2022): 2926, under "Discussion," https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-06799-z. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · full text (CC BY)
- supports: Willendorf at roughly 30,000 years old and Gravettian; the Hohle Fels figurine as the older
  work commonly read as a link to the Gravettian tradition; the regional types from Renancourt in northern
  France to Mal'ta in Siberia
- used by: wh-069

### lubbock-1865 · the coinage
John Lubbock, *Pre-historic Times, as Illustrated by Ancient Remains, and the Manners and Customs of Modern
Savages* (London: Williams and Norgate, 1865), 2–3,
https://archive.org/details/prehistorictime07lubbgoog. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · scanned first edition, public domain
- supports: "This we may call the 'Palaeolithic' period" (2) and "This we may call the 'Neolithic' period"

  the world poorly, made by the man who coined the terms
- used by: wh-005

### larson-2014 · the start of farming
Greger Larson et al., "Current Perspectives and the Future of Domestication Studies," *Proceedings of the
National Academy of Sciences* 111, no. 17 (2014): 6139–46, under "The Origins of Domestication,"
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4035915/. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · full text via PMC
- supports: that plant and animal domestication for food production "began globally 12,000–11,000 y ago at
  the end of the most recent ice age"; at least 11 independent centres of origin
- used by: wh-005, wh-009

### zhu-2021 · what foragers eat
Dan Zhu, Eric D. Galbraith, Victoria Reyes-García, and Philippe Ciais, "Global Hunter-Gatherer Population
Densities Constrained by Influence of Seasonality on Diet Composition," *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 5, no.
11 (2021): 1536–45, under "Seasonality Constrains Diet Composition,"
https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7611941. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · author manuscript in Europe PMC (located by section, not page — the manuscript's
  pagination is not the journal's)
- supports: that where seasonality is low foragers "can subsist mainly on gathering throughout the year",
  while in regions with long non-growing seasons they "depend on hunting to subsist through the season of
  scarcity" — the card's Arctic-versus-warm-places contrast
- used by: wh-009

### smith-codding-2021 · rank without farming
Eric Alden Smith and Brian F. Codding, "Ecological Variation and Institutionalized Inequality in
Hunter-Gatherer Societies," *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 118, no. 13 (2021):
e2016134118, under "Introduction" and "Discussion,"
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8020663/. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · full text via PMC
- supports: that societies "primarily along the North Pacific coast, were characterized by a class system
  of hereditary nobility, commoners, and slaves"; that defensible clumped resources — salmon above all —
  underwrote that hierarchy; and that this happened among foragers, with no farming involved
- used by: wh-009

### hoffman-2017 · Snowball Earth
Paul F. Hoffman et al., "Snowball Earth Climate Dynamics and Cryogenian Geology-Geobiology," *Science
Advances* 3, no. 11 (2017): e1600983, abstract and table 1,
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5677351/. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · full text via PMC
- supports: "grounded ice sheets reached sea level at all latitudes during two long-lived Cryogenian
  glaciations" (abstract); the Sturtian onset at 717–716 Ma and Marinoan deglaciation at ~635 Ma (table 1)
  — the card's "roughly 720 to 635 million years ago"
- used by: wh-011

### pages-2016 · glacials and interglacials
Past Interglacials Working Group of PAGES, "Interglacials of the Last 800,000 Years," *Reviews of
Geophysics* 54, no. 1 (2016): 162–219, under "Defining Interglacials,"
https://hal.science/hal-03245836. [Open access]
- opened: 2026-07-31 · publisher PDF deposited in HAL
- supports: that at the multimillennial scale "the dominant external forcing of Earth's climate is the
  astronomical forcing", which drives glacial–interglacial cycles — the card's point that Milankovitch
  cycles set the beat of the glacials rather than starting an ice age; and the framing of the present as
  the current interglacial
- used by: wh-011

### spratt-lisiecki-2016 · sea level at the LGM
Rachel M. Spratt and Lorraine E. Lisiecki, "A Late Pleistocene Sea Level Stack," *Climate of the Past* 12,
