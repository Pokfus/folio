# Site audit — 2026-08-08

A sweep of the whole project for bugs, obsolete code, undetected flaws and inconsistency. What was fixed
in the same session is at the top; everything below it is planned work, in priority order, with the
measurement that found it so nobody has to re-derive it.

**Method.** All 27 committed regression suites were run (5 dependency-free, 22 under Playwright), plus the
repo's own content audits (`source-audit`, `gloss-source-audit`, `gloss-length`, `check-style`,
`card-focus`, `test-card-plans`) and a set of one-off structural checks over the shipped data. Everything
claimed here was measured, not inferred; where a fault was destructive it was reproduced on a throwaway
copy before being fixed.

### 1 is the one worth remembering

The citation mask added in card batch 23 matches the **card** shape `"sources":[…]`. Glossary citations live
in a top-level `window.GLOSSARY_SOURCES` block with no such key, so **nothing in the glossary was ever
masked** and `--fix` rewrote the titles of real works — Lemos's *The Protogeometric Aegean: The Archaeology
of the Late Eleventh and Tenth Centuries B.C.* came out as *…Late 11th and 10th Centuries B.C.*, and five
others with it. Reproduced on a copy before fixing, and re-verified after: citations untouched, real prose
violations still caught.

**The general lesson: a mask keyed on one file's SHAPE is not a rule about citations.** When a checker grows
a second corpus, re-derive what it must skip there rather than assuming the existing guard travels.

## Batch A — stale translations (2 items, small)

Two pieces of English content were revised after they were translated, and the nine translations kept the
older text. Both are invisible today (`MULTILANG = false`), and both are now *reported* rather than silent.

- **`wh-061`** (behavioural modernity) — English carries 13 markers, every translation carries 14 in a
  different order. The English abstract was rewritten; the translations were not.
- **`Australopithecus`** — the Spanish (and the other eight) are a shorter, earlier description: 5 markers
  against the English's 8. Not a marker bug — a content revision that outran its translations.

**Do not machine-translate these.** They are history content on a study site; either translate properly or
leave them until translations resume. Recorded here so the audits' one-line warnings are not mistaken for
marker-placement faults. `node .claude/source-audit.js` and `node .claude/gloss-source-audit.js` both name
them on every run now.

## Batch B — content outside its own length bar (9 items)

Measured with the pipeline's exact counter (`add-card.js`'s `qWords` + `IMPERIAL_PAREN`), so these are the
same numbers `add-card.js` would refuse.

**Abstracts over 330 words** — `wh-068` (342), `wh-082` (337), `wh-055` (334), `wh-018` (332), `gr-037`
(333), `gr-027` (331), `gr-029` (331).

**Under the floor** — `gr-208` (264 words, 6 short) and glossary `Syracuse` (88 words, bar is 90–110).

The over-length seven predate enforcement (added 2026-08-06) and are recorded in the changelog. `gr-208`
is the interesting one: **`add-card.js` enforces length only at ADD time, and every later editing script —
`add-sources.js`, `fix-field.js`, `add-questions.js` — can push a card out of range in silence.** That is
the card-side version of the gap `gloss-length.js` closed for the glossary.

**Trim and grow carefully, not mechanically.** A trim can strand a footnote marker on a claim it no longer
covers (`add-sources.js` cannot see this — the source is still referenced), and padding to length is the
one way this work can do real damage. Diff the marked sentences' claims either side.

**Suggested tooling, ~40 lines:** `.claude/card-length.js`, the mirror of `gloss-length.js`, so the card bar
is measurable rather than only enforceable. Re-run it after `add-sources.js` and `fix-field.js`.

## Batch C — the changelog has drifted back into transcripts

CLAUDE.md's rule: an item is a summary, and *anything past ~1,000 characters is a transcript*. Two entries
were compressed on request on 2026-08-01. Measured today:

- **26 items over 1,000 characters**, 6 of them over 2,000, the longest **4,278**.
- **11 days break the one-line-per-kind rule** — 2026-08-04 carries four separate "new cards" lines *and*
  four glossary lines; 2026-08-05 announces eleven books across five items.

Reader-facing, so worth doing, but it is editorial work: each merge means reading the day's items and
folding them into one honest line with a raised count. One day per sitting, newest first.

## Batch D — deck-title casing is inconsistent across collections

Branch-deck titles are Title Case in seven collections and sentence case in two (United States 7 of 9
sentence case; China mixed 3/4). Three century decks disagree three ways: *Fourth century and the rise of
Macedon*, *The Severans and the third-century crisis*, *The Nineteenth Century*.

Deliberately **not** changed here: the titles are fixed by the ten collection plans, `test-card-plans.js`
asserts plan-against-tree, and each carries a nine-language `node.i18n` map. Pick one convention, change the
plans and the tree together, and retire the translations of any title that is reworded.

Note the house numbering rule ("centuries are always numbered") is scoped to *card fields and glossary
descriptions*, so deck titles are arguably out of it — `check-style.js` flags them anyway. Decide which, and
say so in CLAUDE.md, so the checker stops reporting two findings nobody intends to act on.

## Performance — the eager path has grown five times over

The documented figure was ~1.4 MB. Measured 2026-08-08:

| file | raw |
|---|---|
| `data.js` | 4.32 MB |
| `app.js` | 1.57 MB |
| `glossary.js` | 1.14 MB |
| `styles.css` | 0.42 MB |
| `changelog.js` | 0.12 MB |
| rest | 0.06 MB |
| **total blocking** | **7.54 MB raw / 2.41 MB gzipped** |

**The single biggest win: 58% of `data.js` (2.06 MB) is the `i18n` blocks of 89 cards, and
`MULTILANG = false` means no reader can reach one byte of it.** This is precisely the `quotes.js` mistake
CLAUDE.md records (27 KB → 312 KB for every visitor) at seven times the scale, and it has the same fix:
move card translations into a lazy `cardI18n:<lang>` bundle beside the glossary's, which is already built
and proven. Roughly: a `DATA_BUNDLES` entry, an ingest hook draining a queue onto `CARD_BY_ID` (the
`glossI18nIngest` shape), `serializeCardData` writing `i18n/cards-<lang>.js`, and `add-card.js` /
`add-lang.js` pointed at it. **`cardLocalized()` already falls back to English**, so a reader whose bundle
has not landed sees English rather than a hole.

Two smaller ones, both safe:

- **`changelog.js` (127 KB) is eager and rendered only on the About page.** `FOLIO_VERSION` is what the home
  page needs, and it is four fields. Split the version out and make `CHANGELOG` lazy.
- **`glossary-wikipedia.js` is an empty stub** (`Object.assign(window.GLOSSARY, {})`) and still costs every
  visitor a blocking request. It is an intentional extension seam, so removing it is a judgement call —
  but it currently does nothing at all.

`GLOSSARY_SOURCES` is a further 479 KB (42% of `glossary.js`) read only once a popup opens; splittable, but
popups are common enough that it is the weakest of the four.

## Repo weight (deploy only, costs readers nothing)

`glossary-wikipedia.json` (2.4 MB) and `countries-info.json` (816 KB) are **resumable caches** for
`fetch-glossary.js` / `fetch-countries.js`, committed to the repo. `.claude/book-cache/` is gitignored for
exactly this reason; these two are not. `admin1.js` (1.7 MB), `ranges.js` and `forests.js` are documented as
kept-for-revival and should stay.

## Where the site is strong (measured, so it does not get "fixed")

- **Citations: 4,028 across cards and glossary — every one carries a URL, an access label, none exceeds the
  600-char trim cap, none trips the closing-parenthesis trap.** Every card is at the 5-source bar and every
  term at the 2-source bar. No card's marker runs past its list; no source goes unreferenced.
- **Data integrity is clean**: no duplicate card or node ids, no card in two decks or none, no orphan rows
  in any of the eight glossary tables, no term without tags, no alias colliding with a term key.
- **`PAGE_META` covers all 20 routes**, and `index.html`'s static baseline matches the home row exactly.
- **Glossary length: 742 of 742 in band bar one** (`Syracuse`).
- **26 of 27 suites green.** `test-csp` reports **0 CSP violations**; its non-zero exit is two blocked
  outbound requests (Google Fonts, Supabase) because headless Chromium does not use the sandbox proxy — an
  environment artifact, not a site fault.

## Suggestions beyond the audit

### Content — the shelf is far ahead of the cards

Two of ten collections are studiable (World History 119, Ancient Greece 215); eight are empty trees behind
1,000-card plans, and China additionally carries `placeholder: true`. **334 cards of a planned 10,000.**
The glossary is 742 terms but only 207 are tagged for Greece and fewer than a dozen for the other seven
subjects — so a new collection starts with almost no glossary behind it.

The highest-leverage choice is **depth over breadth**: finishing Ancient Greece (215 of 1,000, contiguous,
its glossary already the best-served) makes one collection genuinely complete, where opening a ninth tree
adds another empty shelf. Every plan is written so the next card is the lowest unused id, so this needs no
new design.

### Media — a built pipeline with no content in it

**One image across 334 cards and 742 glossary terms.** The site has card images and video, glossary images
and video, a shared fullscreen viewer with zoom and pan, a credit gate that refuses uncredited media, dead-
link handling that hides a broken image from readers and flags it for authors, and a Term-of-the-day plate
that lays out differently when its term has a picture. Museum collection APIs (the Met, already used and
returning 200) are open, permanently addressable and carry their own credit lines. A batch of 50 glossary
images would light up machinery that is already written, tested and paid for.

### Functionality

- **A card-length audit** (Batch B above) — the only house bar with no standing measure.
- **Reading a book is not part of the streak.** The Library is the newest and largest area of the site, and
  a reader who spends twenty minutes in Thucydides has done nothing the account page records. `S.reading`
  already stores per-book position and syncs; a "days read" figure beside "days studied" would cost little.
- **The glossary is reachable only through prose.** 742 cited terms, and the only routes in are a link
  inside a card, a game, or the discovered-terms list behind the account page's meter. There is no way to
  browse or search it. `#glossary` already exists, sorts four ways and filters — opening it to the whole
  glossary rather than just discovered terms is a small change to a page that is already built.
- **`gr-208` under length and `Syracuse` at 88 words** are the two content items a reader could actually
  notice; both are in Batch B.
