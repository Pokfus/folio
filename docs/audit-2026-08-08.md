# Site audit — 2026-08-08

A sweep of the whole project for bugs, obsolete code, undetected flaws and inconsistency, and the record of
what came of it. **All four planned batches are closed**: A dissolved when the translations were removed, B
and C were done, and **D was withdrawn because the finding turned out to be wrong** — see its section, which
is the most useful thing in this file. Each section keeps the measurement that produced it so nobody has to
re-derive it.

**Method.** All 27 committed regression suites were run (5 dependency-free, 22 under Playwright), plus the
repo's own content audits (`source-audit`, `gloss-source-audit`, `gloss-length`, `check-style`,
`card-focus`, `test-card-plans`) and a set of one-off structural checks over the shipped data. Everything
claimed here was measured, not inferred; where a fault was destructive it was reproduced on a throwaway
copy before being fixed.

### 1 is the one worth remembering

The citation mask added in card batch 23 matches the **card** shape `"sources":[…]`. Glossary citations live
in a top-level `window.GLOSSARY_SOURCES` block with no such key, so **nothing in the glossary was ever
masked** and `--fix` rewrote the titles of real works — Lemos's *The Protogeometric Aegean: The Archaeology
of the Late Eleventh and Tenth Centuries B.C.* came out as *…Late 11th and 10th Centuries B.C.*, and five
others with it. Reproduced on a copy before fixing, and re-verified after: citations untouched, real prose
violations still caught.

**The general lesson: a mask keyed on one file's SHAPE is not a rule about citations.** When a checker grows
a second corpus, re-derive what it must skip there rather than assuming the existing guard travels.

## Batch A — stale translations — **DISSOLVED, 2026-08-08**

Both items (`wh-061`, `Australopithecus`) were English revisions that outran their translations. The card
`i18n` blocks and every `i18n/gloss-<lang>.js` were removed on request the same day, so the stale text went
with them and both audits now report **0 markers adrift**. Nothing to do.

## Batch B — content outside its own length bar — **DONE, 2026-08-08**

Seven backgrounds ran 331–342 words and were tightened to 330 or below **by compression alone** — no claim
dropped, and every footnote marker asserted unchanged either side, since a trim can otherwise strand a marker
on a claim it no longer covers and `add-sources.js` cannot see that. `gr-208` (264) and glossary `Syracuse`
(88) were UNDER, and both were **grown from a work already in their own source list** rather than padded: the
BMCR reviews they already cite carry the Selinus/Carthage chain and the Alpheos–Arethusa link the two had left
out. Four curly apostrophes were normalised to the straight form the corpus uses 329 times against 4.

**Standing: 334 backgrounds, 1,002 questions and 742 descriptions, every one inside its bar.**

The root cause is fixed too. `add-card.js` enforced the bars only at ADD time, and every later editing script
writes prose without measuring — `add-sources.js` merges a rewritten abstract, `fix-field.js` does
find-and-replace in any field, `add-questions.js` writes the extras — so a card could drift out of range in
silence, which is what `gr-208` did. **`node .claude/card-length.js`** is the missing measure, mirroring
`gloss-length.js` (`--over` / `--under` / `--questions` / `--prefix=` / `--list`). **Re-run it after those
three scripts.**

## Batch C — the changelog drifting back into transcripts — **DONE, 2026-08-08**

Measured at the start: **26 items over 1,000 characters**, six over 2,000, the longest **4,278**. Now **0
over 1,000**, longest exactly 1,000, and 328 items down to 314. Nothing was dropped — the consolidations fold
what was said across several entries into one. Three days announced books one at a time (eleven books in five
items, nine in nine, five in five) and each now has a single book line; the two citation-batch items that
listed every correction are back to what the 2026-08-01 compression established.

**A measurement correction.** This document originally said 11 days broke the one-line-per-kind rule. That
was a loose regex matching any item CONTAINING "new cards" — including "as many new cards as your decks
actually do". Re-measured against a pattern that only matches an announcement line opening on a count, it was
**1 day**, folded here, and is now **0**. The book-line duplication was the real fault.

## Batch D — deck-title casing — **WITHDRAWN, 2026-08-08: THE FINDING WAS WRONG**

This document claimed branch titles were "Title Case in seven collections and sentence case in two", and that
three century decks disagreed three ways. **Both came from a heuristic that cannot tell a proper noun from
Title Case**, and both are false.

Re-measured by tree level: **level 1 is Title Case in 66 of 66 decks** across all ten collections. The two
apparent exceptions — `Russia since 1985`, `India since 1947` — fail only because *1985* and *1947* have no
capital letter. Leaf decks are sentence case with proper nouns capitalised, and read as mixed only to a
counter: *Persian Wars*, *The Kamakura shogunate*, *The Jacksonian era*, *Manifest Destiny* are all correct
under one scheme. The three century decks each match their own level (`ru-nineteenth` is level 1 and Title
Case; the Greek and Roman ones are leaves and sentence case).

**The convention is real, coherent and already applied: branch decks Title Case, leaf decks sentence case
with proper nouns capitalised.** Nothing to change. The lesson is the one this audit keeps producing — a
crude measure over content invents work; check what it flagged before acting on the count.

The one live remnant: `check-style.js` still reports two century-word findings on deck TITLES, which are out
of the house rule's stated scope (card fields and glossary descriptions). Decide whether deck titles are in
scope and say so in CLAUDE.md, so the checker stops reporting two findings nobody intends to act on.

## Performance — the eager path, before and after — **THE BIG WIN IS TAKEN**

The documented figure was ~1.4 MB and was five times out of date. Measured at the start of this session, and
again after the card and glossary translations were removed on request:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `data.js` | 4.32 MB | **1.64 MB** |
| `app.js` | 1.57 MB | 1.57 MB |
| `glossary.js` | 1.14 MB | 1.14 MB |
| `styles.css` | 0.42 MB | 0.42 MB |
| `changelog.js` | 0.12 MB | 0.12 MB |
| rest | 0.06 MB | 0.06 MB |
| **total blocking** | **7.54 MB raw / 2.41 MB gzipped** | **4.89 MB raw / 1.35 MB gzipped** |

**58% of `data.js` (2.06 MB) was the `i18n` blocks of 89 cards, which `MULTILANG = false` put beyond every
reader's reach** — the `quotes.js` mistake (27 KB → 312 KB for every visitor) at seven times the scale. With
the nine `i18n/gloss-<lang>.js` (3.1 MB of repo weight) it is gone, and **every visitor now downloads about a
megabyte less gzipped before the first card turns over.** `add-card.js` and `add-glossary.js` drop a supplied
translation block with a warning, and `test-i18n-lang.js` fails if a card carries one or a gloss file
reappears, so the eager path cannot regain it quietly.

What remains, in order of size and in decreasing order of worth:

- **`GLOSSARY_SOURCES` is 479 KB, 42% of `glossary.js`**, and is read only once a popup opens. Splittable on
  the same pattern — but popups are common, so this is the weakest of the candidates.
- **`changelog.js` (120 KB) is eager and rendered only on the About page.** `FOLIO_VERSION` is what the home
  page needs and it is four fields. Split the version out and make `CHANGELOG` lazy.
- **`glossary-wikipedia.js` is an empty stub** (`Object.assign(window.GLOSSARY, {})`) and still costs every
  visitor a blocking request. It is an intentional extension seam, so removing it is a judgement call — but
  it currently does nothing at all.

## Repo weight (deploy only, costs readers nothing)

`glossary-wikipedia.json` (2.4 MB) and `countries-info.json` (816 KB) are **resumable caches** for
`fetch-glossary.js` / `fetch-countries.js`, committed to the repo. `.claude/book-cache/` is gitignored for

## Suggestions beyond the audit

### Content — the shelf is far ahead of the cards

Two of ten collections are studiable (World History 119, Ancient Greece 215); eight are empty trees behind
1,000-card plans, and China additionally carries `placeholder: true`. **334 cards of a planned 10,000.**
The glossary is 742 terms but only 207 are tagged for Greece and fewer than a dozen for the other seven
subjects — so a new collection starts with almost no glossary behind it.

The highest-leverage choice is **depth over breadth**: finishing Ancient Greece (215 of 1,000, contiguous,
its glossary already the best-served) makes one collection genuinely complete, where opening a ninth tree
adds another empty shelf. Every plan is written so the next card is the lowest unused id, so this needs no
new design.

### Media — a built pipeline with no content in it

**One image across 334 cards and 742 glossary terms.** The site has card images and video, glossary images
and video, a shared fullscreen viewer with zoom and pan, a credit gate that refuses uncredited media, dead-
link handling that hides a broken image from readers and flags it for authors, and a Term-of-the-day plate
that lays out differently when its term has a picture. Museum collection APIs (the Met, already used and
returning 200) are open, permanently addressable and carry their own credit lines. A batch of 50 glossary
images would light up machinery that is already written, tested and paid for.

### Functionality

- **A card-length audit** (Batch B above) — the only house bar with no standing measure.
- **Reading a book is not part of the streak.** The Library is the newest and largest area of the site, and
  a reader who spends twenty minutes in Thucydides has done nothing the account page records. `S.reading`
  already stores per-book position and syncs; a "days read" figure beside "days studied" would cost little.
- **The glossary is reachable only through prose.** 742 cited terms, and the only routes in are a link
  inside a card, a game, or the discovered-terms list behind the account page's meter. There is no way to
  browse or search it. `#glossary` already exists, sorts four ways and filters — opening it to the whole
  glossary rather than just discovered terms is a small change to a page that is already built.
- **`gr-208` under length and `Syracuse` at 88 words** are the two content items a reader could actually
  notice; both are in Batch B.

- Move the two fetch caches out of the repo (or gitignore them, as `book-cache` is).

  `I18N_HTML` gate now tests any element. Re-audit or mark the document superseded; a stale plan is read as
  a live one.
