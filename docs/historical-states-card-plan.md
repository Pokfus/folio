# Historical States — the card plan

The collection is **Historical States** (`geo-states`), in the Geography SECTION of the Collections page, prefix
**`hs-`**. It was added on 2026-11-19, on request: *"a new geography collection with historical states. The cards
should show an atlas window showing a highlighted state's borders and ask something along the lines of 'What was the
highlighted state in the year [year when greatest extent of borders was reached]'. The card should then inform the
user about the history of that state. The collection does not need a capital or cities deck for now. Instead there
should be several decks for states from different time periods."*

It is **137 numbers in seven decks**, one deck per period, a state filed by the year it is drawn in:

| deck | id | numbers | cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| The first states, to 500 BCE | `geo-states-first` | `hs-001`–`hs-016` | 16 |
| The classical world, 500 – 1 BCE | `geo-states-classical` | `hs-101`–`hs-125` | 25 |
| The age of empires, 1 – 500 CE | `geo-states-empires` | `hs-201`–`hs-219` | 19 |
| The early Middle Ages, 500 – 1000 | `geo-states-early-medieval` | `hs-301`–`hs-319` | 19 |
| The high and late Middle Ages, 1000 – 1500 | `geo-states-medieval` | `hs-401`–`hs-433` | 33 |
| The early modern world, 1500 – 1800 | `geo-states-early-modern` | `hs-501`–`hs-514` | 14 |
| The modern world, 1800 – 1945 | `geo-states-modern` | `hs-601`–`hs-611` | 11 |

There is no capitals deck and no cities deck (the request). **Written so far (2026-11-19): 14 cards**, the first two
of every deck. The next card is the lowest `hs-NNN` of a deck that is not in `data.js`:

    node .claude/next-cards.js hs- 5

## The format: a state of the past as the question

A card is a **map card** (📖 `docs/map-cards.md`) on a layer of its own, **`past-states`**:

```json
"map": { "layer": "past-states", "key": "Roman Empire" }
```

- **The shape is an entity of the Atlas's history** (`atlas/data/history.bin`, Cliopatria's borders), not a shape
  file. `atlas/data/card-faces.js` carries the layer — key → `[entity id, label lon, lat, west, south, east, north,
  YEAR, view lon, view lat, zoom]` — generated by `.claude/atlas-build/build-card-faces.js` (`PAST_LAYERS`), one key
  per polity of the history that is not a 1920 `snapshot` series, **named as the Atlas names it in that year**
  (`polity-names.js`: `Roman Empire` for `pol:rome` at 117, `Eastern Roman Empire` for `pol:byzantium` at 561).
- **The year is the year of the state's greatest extent as the Atlas draws it**, and it is COMPUTED, never typed:
  `entityExtent` in `build-glossary-places.js`, the glossary windows' own rule (the first year in which the area of
  the state's faces is largest), so a term's window and a card's window of one state show the same year.
- **The key is authored and then dropped** like every map card's: `add-card.js` validates it, writes the registry id
  (`pol:rome`) into the card's `places` and removes `map.key`. A studied card therefore discovers its state on the
  reader's Personal Atlas.
- **The window** (`atlas/atlas-windows.js`, `pastLayer`; the layer is flagged `past` in `CARD_MAP_LAYERS`) asks the
  hub for the whole history, as a glossary window's state does, and draws that year: the state in the selection
  gold, every other state of the year plain, the rivers and lakes on. **Nothing is named before the answer.** After
  it, the state is named by the card's own answer and the other states of that year by the Atlas's names
  (`otherStateNames`). It draws no studied siblings and no Collection / All switch: a place studied in another year
  is not on this map.
- **It is never today's map with nothing shaded.** The window keeps its loading state until the history (9 MB) has
  arrived, and where the year cannot be drawn it prints one plain sentence in the window's place: without WebGL2,
  on the file copy of the site (which carries no twin of the whole history), or when the fetch is refused. The card's
  answer and background are unaffected. On such a browser the question cannot be answered from its front; that is
  the format's limit and it is stated here rather than hidden.
- **"Describe geography maps"** (Settings) prints the state's neighbours of that year from
  `atlas/data/card-neighbours.js`: exact, two states that share a border line in that year — among the states the
  Atlas draws, which is fewer than existed (below).

### What every card carries

- **`question`**: `In 117 CE, the state highlighted on the map was <span class="blank">_____</span>.` — the year
  written as the date lines write it (`BCE` / `CE` on every year before 1000). `add-card.js` REFUSES a card whose
  question does not name the layer's year for its key, and `build-card-faces.js --check` (CI) fails when a rebuild
  of `history.bin` moves a state's year until the question is rewritten. No extra phrasings (`"questions": []`).
- **`answer`**: the name a reader would give — the plan line's, which is not always the Atlas's key (`Byzantine
  Empire` on the key `Eastern Roman Empire`, `Song dynasty` on `Southern Song`).
- **`answerDate`**: at most four rows, the last always **`Map year`** — the year the window is drawn in.
- **`facts`**: three to five rows — `Heartland` (the present-day region of its core), `Capital` or `Capitals`, and
  **`Area as drawn`**, the area of the state's faces in that year computed from `history.bin` (`entityExtent`'s
  `km2`; the tables under the list below print it — three significant figures, km² first, `1.39M km² (539k sq mi)`).
  It is the area of THIS map's shape, labelled so, not a historian's estimate, and it is never repeated in the
  background.
- **`abstract`**: the house shape (ten sentences, five and five, 270–330 words, a marker on every sentence), and it
  is the state's HISTORY: block one what the state was — where, when, who ruled it, how it was governed; block two
  its course in order — how it grew to its greatest extent, what it held then, how it broke up or fell. It does not
  mention the map. Where scholarship dates the greatest extent to another year than the map's, the background gives
  the scholarship's year with its source and the date line keeps both.
- **`sources`**: the tiered bar of every new card (difficulty 1 → 9 … 5 → 5). No `why`, no locator, no image (the
  map is the illustration).
- **The glossary term**: the answer's own, written first where it is missing (`add-glossary.js`; grep `glossary.js`
  for the slug before writing).

### The first batch's sourcing rule (2026-11-19)

Every state of the first batch is already the answer of a history card. The fourteen backgrounds were therefore
written **only from claims those cards (and their neighbours) already make, each with the citation the existing
sentence carries** — reworded and re-ordered into the state's history, no claim added. A later batch may research a
state afresh under `docs/card-authoring.md`; a state with no history card must be.

## Which states are in, and which are not

**The collection can only ask what the Atlas can draw.** `history.bin` is not the whole of Cliopatria: it holds the
polities `.claude/polity-spec.json` names — the ones a card somewhere needed — so this plan is every one of those
that is a state with a credible year, and a state the Atlas does not carry (Parthia, the Frankish kingdom, the Holy
Roman Empire, the British Empire…) has no line here. **To add one**: add its series to `polity-spec.json`, rebuild
the history (📖 `docs/atlas-v2-build.md`; `--no-cache`, then the war and Second World War slices), run
`build-card-faces.js --install`, and give it the next number of its deck. For the same reason a window shows fewer
neighbours than a historical atlas would: in 117 CE the Atlas draws Rome and Kush, not Parthia.

**Left out, with the reason** (each is a key of the layer, so `add-card.js` would accept it; the plan does not):

| left out | why |
|---|---|
| Peloponnesian League, Second Athenian League, Athenian Coalition | alliances of states, not a state |
| Etruria | a civilisation of city-states, never one state |
| Chinese Communist base areas, Italian Africa | not states (a movement's territory; a colonial grouping) |
| United States (1776–1800), British Raj (1937–1945), Union of South Africa (1937–1945), Kingdom of Yugoslavia (1937–1945), Mongolian People's Republic (1926–1945), Ethiopian Empire (1900–1935) | the Atlas carries only the years its cards needed, so "the year of greatest extent" inside them is an accident of the cut |
| Finland, Republic of Austria, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Hungary | on today's map under the same name: the World collection's cards |
| Han (state) | its last row is Liu Bang's kingdom of 206–202 BCE, so the Warring State is drawn largest at a size it never had |
| Ptolemaic Egypt | drawn inside Alexander's empire in its first years, so its largest year (326 BCE) is Alexander's shape, not the kingdom's |

The **1920 snapshot series** (`*_1920` in `polity-spec.json`, the League of Nations card's borders) are not keys of
the layer at all.

**Known oddities of the data, kept and to be weighed when the card is written** (a plan line is a subject to
research, not a fact): a state the Atlas draws with ONE shape for its whole life is "largest" in its first year, so
its map year is the first year of its row (the Shang at 1600 BCE, the Zhou at 1000 BCE, the Golden Horde at 1294);
Urartu is drawn largest in 615 BCE, late in its life; the Atlas calls `pol:song` "Southern Song" from 1028 (the
card's answer is `Song dynasty`); the Xiongnu were a steppe confederation, which the card must say. **A card whose
shape or year proves indefensible when its turn comes is deferred here with the reason, not written.**

## How the running order was chosen

Within a deck, **by the area the Atlas draws, largest first**. The largest states of a period are the best known and
the easiest shapes, so the first cards of every deck are the ones a new reader can already half-answer, and a deck
grows harder as it goes; the rule is arithmetic, so nobody has to rank fame. The deck is the period of the MAP YEAR
(the Qing are filed at 1794, in the early modern deck, though the dynasty lasted to 1912).
