# The EU as an External Power — the card plan

The running order for **The EU as an External Power** (`eep`), card prefix `eep-`, across 14 decks:
**Lectures 1–6** and **Readings 1–8**, 300 cards in all. It is the second **course** collection, after
[Politics: East Asia](politics-east-asia-card-plan.md), and it sits in the same **Special** section of the
Collections page for the same reason: it is planned from a syllabus somebody else set, not from a subject.
Read that plan's "What this collection is about" before this one; its rules are inherited here.

## What this collection is about

The course asks whether the European Union is “a sovereign external power or ‘powerless at the global
stage’” (the title of Session 1). Six lectures (Leiden University; Prof. Antoaneta L. Dimitrova, per the
slides) answer it in turn: what kind of power the EU is, and how that idea changed; how CFSP and CSDP grew
from the failed defence community of 1954 to the Strategic Compass; who decides, by which voting rule, and why
that stalls; what regulation and other people's perceptions do to the EU's power; enlargement as a foreign
policy tool; and association and trade agreements as external governance. The eight set readings are
two think-tank commentaries and one news report on European defence, one bank economist's case against
European pessimism, and four academic papers, two of them (Readings 3 and 4) co-written by the lecturer.

It is the **politics of EU external action**, not a history of the Union. A card names an event only where
the course's argument turns on it: Fouchet is here for what its failure did to the shape of EU foreign
policy, not as a card about 1961.

## How the 300 are spread

The other plans fix a running order in advance; this one could, because all fourteen sources arrived together
(10 October 2026). **The numbering is contiguous, lectures first and readings after:**
`eep-001`–`eep-206` are the six lecture decks and `eep-207`–`eep-300` the eight readings. A card id is a permanent address;
the blocks are never renumbered to tidy them, and anything added later goes at `eep-301` upwards, in a deck
appended at the end.

**The volume departs from Politics: East Asia's 30 a lecture and 10 a reading, deliberately.** Lectures here
are not equally dense — Session 1 is mainly framing, Sessions 2 and 3 carry the exam — and the readings
differ more: four of them are academic papers whose frameworks the essays are written in, and one is a news
report. So the count follows what each source holds, and the shortfall in one deck is not made
up by padding it. Lectures take 206 and readings 94.

## Rules these cards follow

- **The answer terms and the load-bearing facts come from the supplied material.** The first 53 cards had
  their backgrounds researched out to the house length and cited like any other card; **from `eep-048` on a
  card uses the supplied material only** (see "Course-only sourcing" below). A card may not assert something
  only a slide asserts without saying it is the lecturer's claim.
- **An answer term may not appear twice, and a lecture and a reading that cover one thing give it to the
  lecture.** The reading's card takes the nearest thing the reading has that the lecture does not: the
  Saint-Malo Declaration (`eep-042`) is Lecture 2's, so Reading 5 takes the wording
  “autonomy rather than independence” (`eep-256`) and the Helsinki Headline Goal
  (`eep-258`).
- **Sixteen cards have an author as the answer, and that is a ceiling.** Where the examinable thing is the
  concept, the concept is the card and the scholar is named in its background; a scholar is a card only
  where the lecture hangs a concept on the name.
- **A date line carries dates, not a fact box**, and where an institution has no date worth memorising the
  line is empty (the lesson of the first hundred East Asia cards).
- **Facts from 2026 and slide labels that differ from the treaties are listed below.** Where the source and
  the slide disagree, the card follows the source and says so.
- **English only, British spelling, metric first.**
- **No card for material that was not supplied.** The second exam essay asks about energy and migration
  deals with authoritarian regimes; the slides give the concepts to answer it with and none of the deals, so
  none is carded (see the exam map).

## Course-only sourcing (from 10 October 2026, on request)

The first 53 cards (`eep-001` to `eep-047` and `eep-207` to `eep-212`) were written under the full house spec: outside
research and five to nine opened sources each. The user then said that for every card not yet built **only the
supplied sources are to be used, with no outside sources and a lowered source minimum, because the collection is for
private study of the university's own material.** So **every card from `eep-048` on is built from the six sets of
lecture slides and the readings alone**, and the importer (`add-card.js`, `src-target.js`, for `eep-` ids only) was
changed to match:

- **One source is the minimum**, where the tiered bar was 5 to 9. A card cites the slides it draws on, tagged
  `[Course material]` and with no URL (they have none), and any reading that covers the term, with the DOI or page it
  was given. The over-citation check ignores `[Course material]` sources.
- **The background is as long as the material supports**: 3 to 10 sentences in one or two blocks, 40 to 330 words, a
  marker on every sentence. The 270-word floor and the strict five-plus-five shape are lifted for these cards, because
  reaching them from a slide that says one line about a term means padding it with facts from outside the course.
- **A card says what the slides say.** Where the slides are wrong, the card attributes the claim to them and the
  correction lives in the ledger below, not in the card. Terms the slides only name get a short card, and a writer
  reports them as thin.
- **The first 53 cards keep the standard they were written to**, so they are the only ones that cite outside sources.

## The exam map

citing the course literature). The slides name four concepts as examples: European Council (`eep-080`); Permanent Structured Cooperation (`eep-102`); Joint decision trap (`eep-074`); Civilian power (`eep-017`).
