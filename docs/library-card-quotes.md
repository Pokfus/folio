# Where a card could quote a Library book

**Asked for in Nov 2026:** *"scan the existing cards in all history and science collections to see where
book quotations could be added."* This is that scan, as it stood on 2026-11-27, generated by
`node .claude/find-card-quotes.js --md=<file>` — re-run it for a fresh list; `--book=<id>` prints what one
book offers (the importer runs that for every new book) and `--prefix=rm-` one collection.

**What a row is.** `card.quote = { book, n, text, cite }` puts a passage of a shelved book under a card, and
`node .claude/add-card-quotes.js <batch.json>` refuses any transcription that is not in the section it
names. A row here is a PLACE TO LOOK — a card, a book and a section — never a passage: choosing the words,
and judging whether the section says anything worth quoting, is the writer's ("the passage is authored,
never extracted"). 1,315 cards already carry a quotation and are not listed.

**Two kinds of candidate.**
- **Cites a shelved book** — the card's own sources open on a book that is on the shelf (author and title;
  a bare "Herodotus 7.210" for the eleven authors of whom a citation can only mean the shelved work), usually
  with the passage's number. The writer has already said where the fact comes from; only the quotation is
  missing. These are the ones to do first. The `§` is the chapter tab the passage falls in.
- **Answer named in a book** — the card's answer is a name a book uses at least twice, the book is not
  older than the card's own date, and the match scores well (how often, in how many chapters, how near in
  time). Weaker: a name is not a passage, and a famous name is everywhere.

**What it cannot see.** A card whose sources cite the work in another edition's words ("Hdt.", "Plut. *Per.*"),
a card about an idea a book discusses without naming, and the modern collections (the Second World War,
Korea, Japan, Biology, Palaeontology), which the shelf has almost nothing to say to: the science shelf is
six books of psychology, Darwin's *Origin* and Euclid.

**The largest openings.** Ancient Egypt: 602 cards cite Breasted's *Ancient Records* or Erman and 23 quote
them — but read `library-audit.md` first: Breasted's text is still an uncorrected scan, and a quotation
from it should be checked against the printed page. Ancient Rome (181) and Ancient Greece (136) are the
cleanest wins: the cards cite Livy, Polybius, Plutarch, Dio, Herodotus, Thucydides, Diodorus, Strabo and
Pausanias by passage. Russia's 82 are the Chronicle of Novgorod and the Primary Chronicle. Psychology's 28
are William James, Thorndike, Pavlov, Ebbinghaus and Freud.

Not part of the site.
