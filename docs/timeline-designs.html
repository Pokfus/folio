Six timeline sections for a card

Each design sits in a Folio study card under the answer box and shows the card beside the two cards before it and the two after it in the same collection, by first date. A period is a continuous coloured bar, a single event a dash, and the card itself is always in the answer term's red. The neighbours are the real ones from data.js.

Rules every design shares

  • Neighbours are the two cards before and the two after in the collection's chronological order (the start year cardStartYear reads from the date line, ties by card id). Nothing else is filtered: a card in the same year counts as a neighbour.
  • Period or event is read from the row of the date line that gave the start year: a range (218 – 201 BCE) is a period and draws a bar; a single date (28 June 1919) is an event and draws a dash. A second row such as In force 10 January 1920 never stretches a bar.
  • The window is the span of the five start dates and the card's own end date, padded 4.5 %. A neighbour's end date widens it only when the extension is no larger than the spread of the five starts; otherwise its bar runs off the right edge with an arrowhead (Kuk Swamp, which runs to 1900 CE, on the Mesolithic card).
  • Scale labels are picked from 1, 2, 5 … 10,000 years, or 1 – 6 months when the window is under three years (the Versailles card).
  • Known wrinkle: the app already reads 10,000 BP as the year −10,000 for sorting, so a BP date and a BCE date sit 1,950 years out of step on the same axis. The real implementation should convert BP rows (subtract 1950) before plotting.
  • Hover any mark or label to connect it with its name; the rest of the section dims.
